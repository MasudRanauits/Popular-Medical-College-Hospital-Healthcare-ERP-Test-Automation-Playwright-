2. Scope and environment

In scope Authentication, module launcher, Patient Registration list and create-patient wizard, New Admission wizard (Patient / Contact Person / Advance Payment / Admission Detail), the admission dashboard — looking an admission up and printing its paperwork — host availability, one end-to-end register-then-admit-then-indent flow, and the five Nurse Station tabs the ward works from: Medicine Indent and its Verify Indent pane, Consultancy Service (the cart, the bill and the cancel-with-reason dialog), IPD Service Entry (the cart, the bill and the entry row, up to but not including the Save — see §20), Diet Indent, and Diet Dashboard (the date range, the search, both grids, and Stop / Continue).
Out of scope OPD, Pharmacy, Diagnostic, HR, Accounts and Billing modules; the ID-card print and the admission dashboard's list-level Print List / Print Package List buttons; what is inside a printed document, which is checked as a valid, non-empty PDF and no further; role-based permissions beyond the Administrator account; performance and security testing.
Environment ERP web application over HTTPS on the hospital host (BASE_URL in .env; self-signed certificate, so ignoreHTTPSErrors is on). Live database — see the warning in section 5.
Browser Chromium (Desktop Chrome profile) at 1920×1080, headed, one worker. Firefox and WebKit projects exist in playwright.config.ts but are commented out; mobile viewports are not enabled.
Test account Administrator account read from ERP_USERNAME / ERP_PASSWORD. Credentials never appear in source control or in this document.
Session handling tests/auth.setup.ts logs in once and saves the browser state to playwright/.auth/user.json. Signed-in suites reuse it; signed-out suites clear it with test.use({ storageState: { cookies: [], origins: [] } }).
Timeouts 90 s per test, 15 s per assertion, 30 s per action, 45 s per navigation — raised from the Playwright defaults because the host is slow. Three cases raise their own further (noted in their rows).

The 126 cases that ran are the setup plus every regression suite. Known fail is not a red build: all 30 are cases marked test.fail(), which Playwright counts as expected. The two red cases of that cycle were DD-02 and DD-13, both BUG-018. Both are green as of 10 Oct 2026 — see the next note. The three skips are not failures: DD-03, DD-04 and DD-08 skip when the ward has not ordered a meal yet, which is the right answer at that hour and not downstream of anything.

Fix pass of 10 Oct 2026 — three red cases, two causes, one of them ours.

  • DD-02 and DD-13 asserted that the application's idea of today equalled the test host's clock. BUG-018 turned out not to be a Diet Dashboard defect at all: the ERP host runs about twelve hours fast — +718 minutes on three consecutive readings, the signature of UTC+6 configured as UTC−6 — so from local noon onwards the app's "today" is tomorrow and both cases failed over something neither is about. They now read the day off the dashboard itself and assert what the tab owns; the clock assertion moved to DD-18, which measures the skew off the host's HTTP Date header and so is wrong at every hour rather than half of them. BUG-018 stays open, retitled, and is now held the way every other defect here is.
  • TC_ADMD_006 was a defect in the automation, not in the ERP. The admission grid filters over the Blazor circuit in a little over 200 ms and keeps the previous rows on screen throughout, and the case's own guard did not cover that gap: every case in that suite seeds itself from the first row of the unfiltered grid, so rowFor(seed) was already visible before the filter had done anything. The case then read ten unfiltered rows, found nine of them in other beds, and failed — and passed whenever the host answered inside the gap, which is why it looked intermittent. searchFor() now waits for the grid to settle on which admissions it lists, not on how many: at ten rows a page, a count cannot tell one set of ten from another.

IPD Service Entry (§20) was run on 06 Oct 2026, after the figures above were taken; the other suites are from the 05 Oct cycle and are unchanged. Its 27 cases ran in two passes — the first was cut short by the test host running out of memory, not by the application, and the remaining cases were run again to completion. Two defects in the automation itself were found and fixed in that first pass and are worth recording, because both were the suite's fault and neither is an application defect: the hidden Service Date input posts 2026-10-06 16:23 for a value the app set and 2026-10-03T10:00:00 for one a nurse typed, so IS-17 was comparing formats rather than moments; and a Service lookup search can be swallowed before Blazor wires the button, leaving the unfiltered catalogue on screen, which failed IS-04 once. The page object now reduces both date shapes to a moment before comparing, and re-sends a search whose grid did not move.

20. Regression — IPD Service Entry

Spec: tests/regression/ipd-service-entry.regression.spec.ts · signed in. Route /hospital/nurse-station, the IPD Service Entry tab — where the ward puts a chargeable service, a procedure or an OT item onto an admitted patient's bill.

How the tab works, since nothing on it says. The ward list fills a patient panel of six read-only boxes — Inv.No, Name, Gender, Age, Assigned Doctor, CabinNo. Under that is one line entry row — Service, Code, Remarks, Service Date, Start Date Time, End Date Time, Rate, Qty — and Enter on Qty is what commits a line: there is no Add button, the only buttons under the cart being Save and Print All. The upper grid is the cart, lines costed but not yet billed, with Total Amount and a discount box under it. The lower grid is the bill, every service already saved against the admission, with who saved it and when.

Nothing in this suite saves, and that is itself a finding. Consultancy Service puts a bin on every row of its bill, which opens a cancel-with-reason dialog — which is how the cases over there can charge a live admission and take the charge back off again. This tab's bill grid has nine columns and no control of any kind on the row: a service saved here cannot be cancelled, corrected or removed from this tab at all. That is IS-24 / BUG-027, and it is also why every case below stops at the cart. A case that pressed Save would be making a permanent change to a real patient's bill with no way to undo it. The one Save the suite does press is IS-25, against an empty cart, which writes nothing and is asserted to be empty first.

Fourteen cases carry known fail. Each asserts the behaviour the tab is supposed to have and is marked test.fail(), so a run reports it as expected-to-fail — and reports it as unexpectedly passing on the day the module is corrected, which is the signal to drop the marker and keep the assertion. Nothing is rewritten to match the defect, which would turn it into a documented feature and leave nothing watching for the fix. The fourteen are raised as BUG-024 … BUG-037 in the companion bug report.
